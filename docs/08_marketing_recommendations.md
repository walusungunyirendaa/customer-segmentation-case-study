## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document turns the five customer segments into practical marketing actions. For each segment it gives one main offer, one channel, and one timing, with the reason each one fits. It also includes a one-page guide for shop staff and a short briefing for customer care. It supports requirements FR12 to FR14 and user stories US9 to US11.

**Scope.** All offers use TelcoX's existing plans and channels. Designing new plans and setting new prices are outside this project. Where an offer needs a discount or bonus, the exact amount is for the product and pricing team to decide.

---

## 2. Principles Behind the Recommendations

1. **Target by need first.** Within every segment, upgrade offers go only to customers who have outgrown their plan. That means customers who use more data or call minutes than their plan allows and are not already on the top plan. There are 7,206 such customers, 48 percent of all customers.
2. **Rank describes concentration, not volume.** The lower-ranked segments still contain about 1,400 such customers each. A need-based filter finds them without sending offers to the rest of the segment.
3. **Match the channel to the segment.** Segments that rarely respond to promotions get personal contact. Segments that respond well get quick digital offers at the moment they top up or hit a limit.
4. **Fewer, better offers.** Customers received about five promotions in six months, and every segment received almost the same number. Offers should now differ by segment, and no customer should receive more than one upgrade or cross-sell offer in a month.
5. **Fit the plan to the customer.** Offer the smallest plan that covers the customer's actual use. A customer who is sent to a plan that is too large is more likely to refuse or to downgrade later.

## 5. Recommendations by Segment

### 5.1 Heavy Data Users (Segment 4)

**Goal:** move customers onto a plan that fits their data use.

**Who to target.** The 1,823 customers who use more data than their plan allows. Of the 1,848 customers in the segment, 731 are prepaid and 1,117 are postpaid. Prepaid customers should be offered the equivalent monthly plan or data bundle.

**Main recommendation**
- **Offer:** the smallest plan that covers their average data use, with a first-month incentive. The plan table makes the rule simple. Standard (15 GB) suits 14 percent of the segment, Plus (40 GB) suits 68 percent, and Premium (100 GB) suits the remaining 18 percent.
- **Channel:** mobile app, using an in-app notification.
- **Timing:** the day the customer reaches their data allowance.

**Supporting actions**
- Show the customer their own usage against their allowance, so the case for upgrading is clear.
- Send an SMS to customers who do not use the app. 42 percent of the segment signed up through the app, so most can be reached there.

**Example message (in-app).** "You used [31] GB of data last month, but your plan includes [5] GB. The Plus plan includes 40 GB. Tap to see the price and switch."

**Why it fits.** 98.6 percent are over their allowance, and they went over in 2.9 of the last 3 months. They have the highest rate of past upgrades (28.4%).

**What to avoid.** Call or text offers. This segment's call use is below average. Also avoid sending the offer in months when the customer was within their limit.

---

### 5.2 Long-Term Voice Users (Segment 5)

**Goal:** move heavy callers onto a plan that covers their calls, and thank them for their long relationship.

**Who to target.** The 1,207 customers who use more call minutes than their plan allows. 884 of the 1,215 in this segment are postpaid.

**Main recommendation**
- **Offer:** a free plan check-up. The agent finds the plan whose call allowance covers the customer's use. By the plan table, Standard (300 minutes) suits 1 percent, Plus (600 minutes) suits 34 percent, and Premium (1,000 minutes) suits 45 percent. The remaining 20 percent use more than 1,000 minutes a month, which is above even the Premium allowance, so they need an extra call-minutes bundle on top of their plan.
- **Channel:** retail shop, with an SMS invitation to visit.
- **Timing:** after a bill showing that the customer went over their call allowance. For prepaid customers, after a top-up that follows a month of heavy calling.

**Supporting actions**
- Customer care can offer the same check-up when these customers call in.
- Use a thank-you tone that mentions how long they have been customers.

**Example message (SMS).** "Thank you for [5] years with TelcoX. You made about [750] call minutes last month. Visit any TelcoX shop for a free plan check-up and we will find the plan that fits your calls."

**Why it fits.** 99.3 percent are over their call allowance, yet only 7.1 percent respond to promotions. A personal conversation in a shop suits that pattern. They are also the oldest segment, with 33 percent aged 55 and over.

**What to avoid.** Data offers, because they use little data (median 3.7 GB). Also avoid pushing the largest plan. Their average spend is the lowest of any segment (17.7), so a big price jump may be refused. Starting from the smallest plan that fits is more likely to succeed.

---

### 5.3 Deal-Seeking Prepaid Users (Segment 3)

**Goal:** turn frequent small purchases into a steadier, higher-value plan.

**Who to target.** The 1,392 customers who have outgrown their plan. 1,168 of them go over their data allowance.

**Main recommendation**
- **Offer:** bonus data when the customer buys a larger monthly bundle, as a step towards a monthly plan. The offer should be time-limited, because this segment is used to responding to deals.
- **Channel:** USSD and self-service.
- **Timing:** at the moment they top up, which happens about 4.7 times a month.

**Supporting actions**
- Use SMS and in-app messages on the same day as the top-up offer.
- Resellers sell a lot of top-ups to this group (28% signed up through resellers). Resellers can mention the offer at the point of sale.

**Example message (USSD).** "Top up [10] or more and get bonus data on a monthly bundle. Reply 1 to see the offer."

**Why it fits.** They respond to promotions at 27.4 percent, which is the highest of any segment. They buy about 5 bundles in three months and go over their data limit in 1.2 of the last 3 months.

**What to avoid.** Offers every week, because this group may learn to wait for the next deal. Also avoid long contracts. They are the newest customers (1.8 years on average) and prefer small commitments.

---

### 5.4 Steady Postpaid Households (Segment 1)

**Goal:** grow the value of a loyal, stable segment by adding services, instead of pushing upgrades.

**Who to target.** The 3,165 customers without home broadband. Two smaller groups can be handled as extra actions: 733 customers who roam abroad on three or more days in three months, and 1,420 customers who have outgrown their plan.

**Main recommendation**
- **Offer:** a home broadband and mobile bundle.
- **Channel:** retail shop.
- **Timing:** at the next shop visit or customer care contact.

**Supporting actions**
- Roaming customers: offer a roaming add-on when their first roaming day is detected. They spend about 42.5 a month against 30.7 for the rest of the segment.
- Customers who have outgrown their plan: offer a plan check-up at the same visit.

**Example script (shop).** "Many of our long-standing customers have added home broadband to their mobile plan. Would you like to hear what it would cost for you?"

**Why it fits.** This segment has the highest broadband take-up (38.4% against 21.9% overall), the longest-standing postpaid relationships (3.5 years), and a low response to promotions (9.2%). A personal offer at a moment when the customer is already in contact is more likely to work than a broad message.

**What to avoid.** Broad SMS blasts. This is the largest segment, and a low response rate would waste a lot of spend. Most of its customers (72%) are within their plan limits, so an upgrade push does not fit.

---

### 5.5 Light Prepaid Users (Segment 2)

**Goal:** serve the small group with a clear need at low cost, and keep the rest lightly engaged.

**Who to target.** The 1,364 customers who have outgrown their plan. Most of them are over their call allowance (1,152 customers), and 799 of those are on the Basic plan with its 100-minute limit.

**Main recommendation**
- **Offer:** a plan with a larger call allowance, such as Standard (300 minutes), for customers over their current call limit.
- **Channel:** USSD and self-service.
- **Timing:** at the moment they top up.

**Supporting actions**
- Keep contact light for the rest of the segment. Do not run broad upgrade campaigns here.
- Resellers (26% of the segment) can pass on the offer in person.

**Example message (USSD).** "You used [250] call minutes last month. The Standard plan includes 300. Reply 1 to learn more."

**Why it fits.** The typical customer uses only about a third of their data allowance, so the need is on calls. Response to promotions is the lowest of any segment (4.9%), so a low-cost channel keeps the spend small.

**What to avoid.** Data offers and expensive campaigns. This segment spends little (19.9) and rarely upgrades (9.4%), so heavy investment is unlikely to pay back.

## 6. Campaign Priority and Order

| Order | Segment | Customers targeted | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Heavy Data Users | 1,823 | Clearest need and the highest upgrade potential. Quick to run in the app. |
| 2 | Deal-Seeking Prepaid Users | 1,392 | Best response rate, and the offer reaches them at the moment of top-up. |
| 2 | Long-Term Voice Users | 1,207 | Strongest need, but a slower, personal approach through shops. Run at the same time as the deal-seekers. |
| 3 | Steady Postpaid Households | 3,165 | Growth through broadband. Largest group, so start with shop-based offers and review results before widening. |
| 3 | Light Prepaid Users | 1,364 | Lowest priority. Run a small, low-cost campaign. |

The order follows the upgrade ranking in document 07, with segment 1 treated as a cross-sell campaign. Document 09 explains how the campaigns will be tested before they are widened.

## 7. Retail Quick Guides

One short guide per segment for shop staff (requirement FR14, user story US10). Staff can see a customer's plan and usage when they look up the account.

### Heavy Data Users
- **How to recognize them:** uses far more data than the plan allows, often on a Basic or Standard plan.
- **Opening line:** "I can see you have been using a lot of data. Let me show you a plan that would cover it."
- **Offer:** the smallest plan that covers their data, with the first-month incentive.
- **Avoid:** talking about calls or texts.

### Long-Term Voice Users
- **How to recognize them:** a long-standing customer with call minutes well above the plan allowance.
- **Opening line:** "Thank you for being with us so long. Let's check whether your plan still suits the way you use it."
- **Offer:** the plan check-up, moving to the plan that covers their calls. Add an extra call-minutes bundle for those over 1,000 minutes.
- **Avoid:** pushing the biggest plan, or leading with price.

### Deal-Seeking Prepaid Users
- **How to recognize them:** top-ups several times a month, many bundles, usually younger.
- **Opening line:** "I can see you often buy bundles. There is a bigger monthly bundle with bonus data that may save you trips."
- **Offer:** bonus data on a larger monthly bundle.
- **Avoid:** long contracts.

### Steady Postpaid Households
- **How to recognize them:** postpaid for several years, on a mid-range plan, within their limits.
- **Opening line:** "Many of our long-standing customers have added home broadband. Would you like to hear what it would cost for you?"
- **Offer:** home broadband and mobile bundle. Mention the roaming add-on to customers who travel.
- **Avoid:** upgrade pressure. Their plan usually fits.

### Light Prepaid Users
- **How to recognize them:** low use, small top-ups, Basic plan.
- **Opening line:** "I can see you make quite a few calls. A plan with more call minutes might suit you."
- **Offer:** a plan with a larger call allowance, only if they exceed their current limit.
- **Avoid:** data offers.

## 8. Customer Care Briefing

Likely questions after each campaign (requirement US11). In every case, the first step is to show the customer their last three months of usage next to their plan allowance.

| Segment | Likely questions | How to help |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy Data Users | "Why did I run out of data so quickly?" "Do I lose my remaining data if I switch part-way through the month?" | Explain allowance against usage. Check the switching rules before promising anything. |
| Long-Term Voice Users | "Can I keep my number and my current plan if I say no?" "Is there a plan that covers all of my calls?" | Confirm that declining changes nothing. Explain the call allowance of each plan, and the extra call-minutes bundle. |
| Deal-Seeking Prepaid Users | "What happens to my bonus data when it expires?" "Do I have to keep topping up to keep the offer?" | State the end date and conditions of the bonus clearly. |
| Steady Postpaid Households | "Is broadband available at my address?" "Will adding broadband change my mobile bill?" | Check availability first. Explain what is billed together and what is separate. |
| Light Prepaid Users | "Why am I being offered a plan?" "Will my top-ups work the same way?" | Show their call use against the Basic allowance, and explain that top-ups work as before. |
