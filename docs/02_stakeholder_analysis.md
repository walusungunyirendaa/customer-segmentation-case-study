## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document looks closely at the people and teams affected by the customer segmentation project. For each one, it explains what they need, how they will use the results, what worries them, and how the project team should keep them involved.

Knowing this early helps the project deliver segments that people will actually use.

| ID | Stakeholder | Role in the project | Main interest |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Marketing team | Main user of the segments | Better campaigns and higher response |

| S3 | Sales and retail team | Uses segments with customers face to face | Simple guidance for upgrade conversations |

## 3. Stakeholder Details

### S1. Marketing team

- **What they need:** Clear customer groups they can target, with simple descriptions and suggested offers.
- **How they will use the results:** To plan campaigns, choose who gets which offer, and decide where to spend the budget.
- **Main concerns:** Segments that are too complicated, too many groups to manage, or groups they cannot reach through current channels.
- **What success looks like to them:** Higher response to promotions and less money spent on offers that do not fit.

### S2. Product and pricing team

- **What they need:** To see whether the current plans match how customers really use their services.
- **How they will use the results:** To spot gaps between plans and customer needs, and to consider changes to plans in the future.
- **Main concerns:** Results that suggest changes to plans that are hard to carry out.
- **What success looks like to them:** Clear evidence about which customer groups need a different plan.

### S3. Sales and retail team

- **What they need:** Short, simple segment descriptions they can use in shops and calls.
- **How they will use the results:** To recognize which customers may be ready to upgrade and to offer the right plan.
- **Main concerns:** Long reports, technical language, or extra steps that slow down service.
- **What success looks like to them:** More successful upgrade conversations with less effort.

### S4. Customer care team

- **What they need:** To know what problems and questions are common in each segment.
- **How they will use the results:** To prepare for questions about new offers and to improve how they help each group.
- **Main concerns:** A rise in complaints if customers receive offers that do not suit them.
- **What success looks like to them:** Fewer complaints about irrelevant messages.

### S5. IT and data team

- **What they need:** A clear list of data fields, where they come from, and how often they are needed.
- **How they will use the results:** To prepare the data and, later, load segment labels into the systems that marketing uses.
- **Main concerns:** Heavy extra workload, unclear data requests, and data quality problems.
- **What success looks like to them:** A clear request, a clean process, and results that are easy to maintain.

### S6. Leadership

- **What they need:** Proof that segmentation improves business results.
- **How they will use the results:** To decide whether to continue, expand, or fund the approach.
- **Main concerns:** Cost, delays, and unclear benefits.
- **What success looks like to them:** Growth in upgrades and ARPU, and a better return on marketing spending.

### S7. Legal and compliance team

- **What they need:** Confidence that customer data is used lawfully and safely.
- **How they will use the results:** To review what data is used and how segments are stored and shared.
- **Main concerns:** Use of sensitive personal details, and data being shared with people who should not see it.
- **What success looks like to them:** A project that follows data protection rules with no complaints.

### S8. Customers

- **What they need:** Offers that fit their needs and respect for their privacy.
- **How they will be affected:** They will receive more relevant promotions and fewer unwanted messages.
- **Main concerns:** Too many messages, offers that feel pushy, and how their data is used.
- **What success looks like to them:** Offers that are useful and easy to understand.

## 4. Influence and Interest

This table rates how much power each stakeholder has over the project (influence) and how much they care about the result (interest). The ratings are High, Medium, or Low.

| Stakeholder | Influence | Interest | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leadership | High | High | Manage closely |
| Marketing team | High | High | Manage closely |
| Product and pricing team | Medium | High | Keep informed and involved |
| IT and data team | High | Medium | Keep satisfied |
| Legal and compliance team | High | Low | Keep satisfied |
| Sales and retail team | Medium | Medium | Keep informed |
| Customer care team | Low | Medium | Keep informed |
| Customers | Low | High | Protect their interests |

### What the priorities mean

- **Manage closely:** Involve them often and ask for their input on key decisions.
- **Keep satisfied:** Make sure their needs are met, and update them at important points.
- **Keep informed:** Share results and changes regularly.
- **Protect their interests:** They are not part of the team, but decisions should consider how they are affected.

## 5. Where Stakeholder Needs May Conflict

Different teams want different things. Knowing this early helps avoid problems later.

| Possible conflict | Teams involved | Suggested way forward |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing wants many detailed segments, while sales wants only a few simple ones. | Marketing and Sales | Aim for a small number of clear segments and agree on the final number together. |
| Marketing wants to use as much customer data as possible, while legal wants to limit it. | Marketing and Legal | Use only the data that is needed, and review the data list with legal early. |
| Leadership wants quick results, while IT needs time to prepare clean data. | Leadership and IT | Agree on a realistic timeline and share progress at set points. |
| Teams define a "high-value customer" differently. | Marketing, Sales, Product | Agree on one shared definition at the start of the project. |

| Stakeholder | What to share | How | How often |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leadership | Progress summary, key findings, expected business impact | Short summary and presentation | At each major step |
| Marketing team | Draft segments, profiles, and offer ideas | Workshop and written profiles | Every week |
| Product and pricing team | Usage findings and plan gaps | Meeting and short report | At key steps |

| Legal and compliance team | Data list and privacy approach | Written review | Before analysis begins and before results are shared |
| Sales and retail team | Simple segment guide | One-page summary | When segments are final |
| Customer care team | Segment summary and expected questions | Short briefing | Before campaigns start |

## 7. Stakeholder Questions to Ask

These questions will guide early conversations with each team.

**Marketing**
- Which campaigns run today, and which results do you track?
- How many segments can your team realistically manage?
- Which channels can you use to reach customers?

**Product and pricing**
- Which plans are the most and least popular?
- What do you believe stops customers from upgrading?

**Sales and retail**
- What do customers ask for before they upgrade?
- What information would help you most during a conversation?

**Customer care**
- Which complaints are linked to promotions or plan changes?

**IT and data**
- Which systems hold the data, and how can it be combined?
- How often is the data updated?

**Leadership**
- What improvement would make this project a success?
- What is the time and budget limit?

**Legal and compliance**
- Which customer details are allowed in this analysis?
- What rules apply to storing and sharing segment labels?

---

## 8. Summary

The most important stakeholders are leadership and the marketing team, so they will be involved most closely. IT, legal, and product teams have a strong effect on what is possible, so they will be consulted early. Sales, customer care, and customers are affected by the results, so their needs will shape how segments are described and how offers are made.

The main points to settle early are the number of segments, one shared meaning of "high-value customer," and which customer data can be used.
